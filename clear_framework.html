The CLEAR Framework

A leadership decision-making framework for better choices under pressure — by Margie Lynch, MA, PCC, CAPP

Begin →
Overview

Executive Summary

The CLEAR Framework is a structured, five-step model designed for leaders who must make sound decisions when stakes are high and time is short. Developed from real-world leadership experience during organizational restructuring, the framework is grounded in neuroscience research showing that stress changes how our brains process information — narrowing focus, increasing emotional reactivity, and defaulting to familiar patterns. CLEAR provides a systematic antidote to these cognitive pitfalls while remaining fast enough for pressure situations.

The Five Steps

The CLEAR Framework

A decision-making system designed for the moments that define your leadership — when time is short, stakes are high, and people are watching.

Clarify the Real Issue
"What problem am I actually solving?"
Pressure creates urgency around symptoms rather than root causes. Sometimes the urgent issue isn't the important one. Misdiagnosing the problem guarantees a misguided solution. This step forces a pause for accurate problem identification.
List Your Options
Force at least three alternatives
Under pressure, we often see only two choices: do something or do nothing. Research shows that considering multiple alternatives improves decision quality — even when you ultimately choose one of your original options. A third option unlocks creative thinking.
Evaluate Impact and Risk
Who will be affected? What could go wrong?
For each option, quickly assess: Who will be affected? What are potential positive outcomes? What could go wrong? What would happen if we don't decide now? This rapid triage prevents both recklessness and paralysis.
Align with Values and Goals
"Which option best serves our people and purposes?"
Values-based decisions create less regret, even when outcomes are challenging. When a decision aligns with core principles and long-term objectives, it remains defensible regardless of short-term results.
Respond with Confidence
Commit fully and communicate clearly
Communicate the decision itself, your reasoning, acknowledged uncertainties, and next steps. A well-made decision poorly communicated often fails. A decent decision communicated with confidence and clarity often succeeds.
Supporting Tools

Practical Instruments

Companion techniques that enhance the CLEAR framework and make it operational under real pressure.

📊
The Information Sweet Spot
Make decisions when you have 50–70% of the information you wish you had.
  • Below 50% → you're guessing
  • Above 70% → you're overthinking
  • Mirrors Bezos's famous threshold
⏱️
The 60-Second Reset
A pre-decision ritual that shifts you from reactive to responsive mode:
  • Take a deep breath
  • "I have the skills and judgment needed"
  • "What outcome do I want to create?"
  • Apply CLEAR quickly
  • Decide and act
👥
Quick Stakeholder Scan
Even under time pressure, assess impact across four dimensions:
  • Team — morale, work, development
  • Clients — experience, outcomes
  • Organization — culture, strategy
  • You — personal & professional
Continuous Growth

Post-Decision Learning

The goal isn't perfect decisions — it's better decisions over time. Resilient leaders learn from both good and poor outcomes.

Monthly Decision Review
  • What information was most helpful in deciding?
  • What biases might have influenced the decision?
  • What would you do differently with the same information?
  • What patterns do you notice in your decision-making?
The Recovery Framework
  • Acknowledge — what happened, without defensiveness
  • Identify — lessons learned for future decisions
  • Adjust — course-correct quickly with new information
  • Maintain — confidence in your decision-making ability
Core Principles

Key Insights

🧠
Learnable Skill
Decision-making under pressure is a skill, not a personality trait. You can systematically improve with practice.
⚖️
Perfectionism is the Enemy
"Good enough now" beats "perfect later." Leadership decisions aren't about finding perfect solutions.
🚫
Indecision is a Choice
Usually the worst one. It creates the least positive impact. Every choice means saying no to alternatives.
🤝
Teams Need Decisiveness
They don't need you to always be right. They need you to be thoughtful, decisive, and learning-oriented.
⏸️
Pause Improves Speed
A brief pause improves both speed AND quality. Reactive ≠ responsive. Shift from reaction to intention.
💛
Courage Builds Trust
Courageous decisions made with empathy build trust, even when outcomes are challenging.

Final Thought

The Greatest Insight

The CLEAR framework fills a critical gap: it is explicitly designed for the emotional and temporal pressure of real leadership moments — the client crisis, the team conflict, the surprise opportunity. Where Musk excels in analytical rigor and Bezos in organizational velocity, CLEAR centers on human impact, value alignment, and confident communication. The quality of a decision is determined not only by the choice made, but by how it is communicated and how the leader responds when outcomes diverge from expectations.
